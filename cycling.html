Sleeping Where the Road Ends

There is a peculiar silence at 2,000 meters. No cars, no birds, just the faint whistle of wind through guy-lines. I pitched my tent on a shelf of grass overlooking Balea Lake, ate cold beans straight from the can, and watched the stars emerge one by one until the sky looked like shattered glass. The descent the next morning — 25 km of perfect tarmac switchbacks — was my reward. I did not touch the brakes once for the first ten kilometers.

My First Real Bikepacking Trip

I overpacked. Dramatically. My handlebar bag weighed as much as a small child and my rear rack groaned under camping gear I never touched. But I learned. I learned that a 500g sleeping bag is not warm enough in May. I learned that instant coffee tastes like victory at 6 AM. And I learned that the best conversations happen with strangers at remote mountain huts who cannot believe you cycled there.
